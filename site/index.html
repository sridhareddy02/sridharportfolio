About

I'm a marketing and data analyst who likes the part of the job where a messy pile of campaign, customer and revenue data turns into a clear recommendation. At Procter & Gamble I build and maintain the dashboards, KPI definitions and QA checks behind campaign reporting, and I run the segmentation and A/B testing that shape optimization recommendations.

Before that I spent three years at Aspire TechnoLab in Hyderabad, first as a marketing analytics intern and then as a marketing and data analyst, building SQL ETL workflows, Tableau and Power BI reporting, and Python models for forecasting and segmentation.

In between I completed an M.S. in Computer Science at Indiana University Bloomington, where I worked on a healthcare research-data project (FADS) and built an AI assistant (AURA).

I also build practice projects on simulated data to sharpen the methods I use at work. Three of them are below, each with tests and an honest list of limitations.

02 / Projects

Things I have built

Three practice projects that mirror the work above. They run on simulated data with known answers, so every method can be checked, and each repository has tests, documentation and stated limitations.

Project-1Simulated data

Marketing attribution and ROI lab

Six attribution models, a SQL KPI layer and a randomised holdout test, all scored against a simulation where the true effect of every channel is known.

  • The Markov-chain model was closest to the truth (5.6 percentage points of average error); first touch was furthest (7.2).
  • Every model gave email about 20% of the credit. Its true incremental share was 10%.
  • A 10% holdout recovered paid social's incremental ROAS (1.30, 95% CI 0.76 to 1.84, against a true 1.40) while last touch said 1.02.

Related experience: multi-touch attribution, campaign measurement and ROI work at P&G.

Project-2Simulated data

Customer lifecycle and experimentation lab

Segmentation, cohort retention, churn and value models tested on a later time period, a revenue forecast, and an A/B testing toolkit that checks its own statistics.

  • Champions are 23% of customers but 54% of revenue; 57% of them buy again within 90 days against 5% of hibernating customers.
  • Churn model trained on one snapshot and tested on a later one: AUC 0.70. Revenue forecast error 1.9% MAPE against 17.3% for a naive forecast.
  • The A/B toolkit covers sample-size planning, CUPED, sample-ratio checks and false-discovery control, and was validated on 4,000 simulated experiments (5.1% false positives on A/A tests).

Related experience: customer segments, behavioral cohorts, retention and A/B testing at P&G.
